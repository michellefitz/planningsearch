Option A · leading candidate

Citation

The prose does the analysis. The card is the receipt — the record being cited, and the way into it. It names the address too, in the quiet line, so a reader never has to work out which paragraph a card belongs to.

As rendered

33 Rossberry Avenue — refused in 2024 for a combined single/two-storey side extension plus a new pedestrian gate. Reasons: the reduced private open space would amount to overdevelopment (contrary to Policy H9), the new side gate threatened pedestrian safety on the footpath, and no SuDS drainage measures were proposed.

And when three are cited in a row

The three South Dublin refusals turn on the same two things — open space and drainage.

Stacked, they read as a source list — and each one says which property it is.

Why

The status and the proposal stop being repeated, and the address stays as an anchor. The card is short enough to sit under every mention without swamping the answer, and it gains the decision date — a fact the prose rarely carries.

Tradeoff

The address is said twice — deliberately, since the alternative is guessing which paragraph a card belongs to. It is quiet and on one line, so it reads as a label rather than a second headline.
